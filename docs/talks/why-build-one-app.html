What it is

A template you're allowed to take

ScriptHammer is a production Next.js 15 template. Not a starter you throw away at week two — the thing my own client sites run on.

What comes in the box

  • Next.js 15 · React 19 · TypeScript strict
  • Supabase auth, database, realtime
  • PWA, offline, service worker
  • 35 themes — 31 AAA, 4 AA

What's unusual

  • 4,787 unit tests across 452 files
  • E2E on a Supabase per runner, 3 browsers on merge
  • 7 checks must be green to merge
  • Docker-first — no "works on my machine"
ScriptHammer is the template my own client sites run on. That matters more than the feature list — it is not a demo I maintain for talks, it is the thing that breaks at two in the morning if I get it wrong. The right-hand column is the unusual part: most templates do not hand you their CI arguments already settled.

While it builds · the real lesson

Most of my bugs were green checks

Not failures. Successes that measured nothing.

A test asserting nothing

if (await errorBanner.isVisible()) {
  await expect(retryButton).toBeVisible();
}
// passes when the banner never appears

An account delete that deleted nothing

No delete policy on the table. The database filtered the request to zero rows and returned no error. The app said "permanently deleted" and signed you out.

Here is the uncomfortable middle of the talk. Almost every serious bug I found last year was not a failing test. It was a passing one that measured nothing. On the left, an assertion inside a condition that is false every run. On the right, an account deletion that deleted nothing, returned no error, and told the user their data was permanently gone. And the two panels are one story: the left-hand work is what surfaced the right-hand bug. Once the assertion count was real, #859 was open and fixed inside two hours.

While it builds · the fix

Count the assertions, not the checkmarks

[assertion-count] 4 of 17 passing test(s) ran ZERO assertions (#396):
  accessibility.spec.ts:23 › homepage passes automated checks
  cross-page-navigation.spec.ts:166 › external links open in new tab
  …

# nine of these across 24 shards and three browsers.
# now merge-blocking on the required lane (#861), in the template you just forked.

One of those nine was hiding the delete bug. Nine tests, months of green, one real data-protection defect underneath. The reporter prints “#396” — that is the catalogue of gates that could not fail. The gate itself is #861.

So stop counting checkmarks and start counting assertions. Nine passing tests across twenty-four CI shards had never asserted anything at all, and one of those nine was hiding the delete bug. That is now a merge-blocking gate, and it is in the template you just forked — you get it for free.
