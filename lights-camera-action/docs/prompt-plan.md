# Kids' game — Base44 PROMPT PLAN (written Nov 7 after the first build was scrapped)

## What went wrong, so it stays written down
The first Base44 build was scrapped by Jonathan: flat 2D look, aquarium on the
wrong bank, placeholder name, and the prompts were spent reactively — build,
then a 3D fix, then a queued scenery fix he had to stop. Three prompts of
fragmented feedback instead of one intentional plan. The failed app gets
parked (renamed ARCHIVED, left unpublished, never submitted).

## The rules from here
1. Every prompt is written out in advance, in this plan, before anything is
   submitted. No prompt fires without this plan + Jonathan's go.
2. **NO prompt is ever sent without Jonathan reading its exact text first**
   (Jonathan, Nov 7, 10:00 AM). The plan being approved is not enough — he
   reads the actual words that will go in, every time.
3. Foundational things go FIRST and TOGETHER. Feedback between stages is
   logged (kids-game-feedback-batch.md), never fired piecemeal.
4. Attachments are capped at 10 files per Base44 message — plan the count.
5. The game NAME is picked before Prompt 1: the prompt's title line becomes
   the app name. No placeholder names ever again.
6. **Orientation is LOCKED to landscape** (Jonathan's ruling, Nov 7, 1:40 PM).
   No portrait layout, no "rotate your device" screen, in any prompt. Every
   layout is landscape-only, so the bottom dock is always a landscape strip.
7. **NO ASSUMPTIONS IN PROMPTS.** Every line in a builder prompt traces to
   (1) Jonathan's actual words or rulings, (2) a design he approved, or (3)
   data as delivered. Restrictions, limitations, and exclusions he never
   stated do not go in — anything uncertain is pulled into a visible
   'Assumptions — his call' list at the top of the draft, never buried in
   the body. (Nov 7: wordless Chorus, Crash's four-sound kit, and 'never
   lift off the deck' were all assumptions written in his name; he caught
   every one.)

## The prompt map — 3 planned prompts + 1 reserve for the whole game

### Prompt 1 — THE PLACE + THE BAND (one prompt, everything foundational)
- Real-time 3D (Three.js / WebGL). One fixed camera at the soundboard on the
  lawn — we stay in that one spot, but real perspective depth throughout.
- Chattanooga geography, spelled out so it can't be gotten wrong:
  Ross's Landing is on the SOUTH (downtown) bank of the Tennessee River.
  Camera on the lawn facing the stage; the river directly BEHIND the stage.
  Across the river: the North Shore — low, tree-covered hills and houses,
  NO aquarium, NO downtown over there. **The Tennessee Aquarium is NOT in
  this scene at all** (corrected twice by Jonathan, final word Nov 7,
  10:00 AM): at the soundboard angle it sits behind the camera, up the bluff
  on the downtown side — you'd never see it. It does not appear in the game
  view: not across the river, not behind the stage, nowhere. Same for the
  downtown towers — behind the camera, out of frame. Bridges crossing to
  the sides of the view (Walnut Street pedestrian bridge, Market Street
  bridge) — match the attached photos. Golden-hour sky, soft natural
  photographic clouds — no cartoon blobs.
- The stage matches the attached photos: flat canopy roof, back wall, light
  row under the canopy, speaker stacks both sides, river behind.
- ALL EIGHT robots on the deck as rounded 3D figures, each built around
  their instrument, matched to the attached turnaround sheets: Crash
  (drums), Timber (upright bass), Chops (guitar), Reed (saxophone), Ivory
  (piano), Brass (trumpet), Chorus (singer), Switch (synth board). Each one
  animates when their instrument sounds.
- Attachments (exactly 10): ross-stage-fullres.jpg, ross-stage-closeup.jpg,
  and all 8 turnaround sheets.
- Deliberately NOT in Prompt 1: the editors. Scene + band, done right, first.

### Prompt 2 — THE SOUNDBOARD (one prompt)
Channel strip per musician (portrait, mute, Open). Crash gets the beatbox
grid (deep drum / snap / tick × 16 steps). The seven melodic instruments get
REAL five-line staffs with the right clef each, 16 steps, tap to place/remove,
drag to move, note names shown, ~octave-and-a-half range. Each instrument
sounds its own browser synth at the written pitch. Play/Stop, tempo slider,
sweep marker, robots animate on their notes. One complete song pre-programmed
across all 8 channels.

### Prompt 3 — THE SUN + POLISH (one prompt)
The Sun panel (Shelly's light board, first light): speed, color, angle/height,
brightness. The sun is the 3D scene's actual light source — sky, stage light
and shadows follow it; during Play it arcs down behind the stage paced to the
music unless the kid grabs it. Plus whatever polish sits in the feedback log
by then — batched, all of it, in this one prompt.

### Prompt 4 — RESERVE (unspent unless needed)
Post-team-meeting edits and submission prep. If it isn't needed, it never
gets spent.

matching the game's three AV boards (lights, cameras, and the show itself
